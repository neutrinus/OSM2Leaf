## Question

Does the 16-byte row hypothesis hold over the whole file, and does the
`src`/`dst` reading yield a reciprocal graph (every `dst` appears as a `src`)?

## Method

Decoded 16-byte rows from `0x1000` with `tools/rdstm_record.py` layout
(`id,pad2,ref,src,dst,code,tail`). Computed reciprocity (dst ∈ src set) for
the first 10 rows and a shuffled-src control. Inspected bytes around the
documented drift at `0x10a0`.

## Observations

- Rows `0x1000`–`0x1090` (10 rows) decode byte-cleanly as 16-byte records,
  but their `src` set is `{0,180,256,512}` while `dst` values are
  `{2304,560,1119,992,798,1354,1923,1965,744,907}` — **0/10 reciprocal**.
  Shuffled control also 0/10.
- At `0x10a0` the 16-byte framing breaks: `id` jumps (512, 700, 1343, …),
  `src`/`dst` become incoherent and the stream reaches all-zero padding at
  `0x1220`.
- `rdstm_schema.py scan` ranks a 2-byte record at block `0x400` highest
  (score 0.371), with `multiple of 2` notes, i.e. the 16-byte framing is not
  the best fit and is likely a multiple of a smaller unit.

## Interpretation

The 16-byte row hypothesis (C-106/HYPOTHESIS) does not produce a reciprocal
graph even on its first 10 rows, and the framing drifts at `0x10a0`. The
record size is therefore not a uniform 16 bytes; the leading `0x1000`..`0x10a0`
block (10 × 16 bytes) is at best a header/table, and the main record stream
begins later or uses a different stride. M4.16 (row→tile placement) is
blocked on a corrected framing.

## Claims

- C-1800: 16-byte rows from `0x1000` decode byte-cleanly but yield 0/10
  reciprocity (src set {0,180,256,512} vs dst {2304,560,…}); shuffled
  control 0/10.
- C-1801: framing drifts at `0x10a0` (after 10 rows) and reaches zero
  padding at `0x1220`.
- C-1802: `rdstm_schema.py scan` prefers a 2-byte record (score 0.371) over
  16 bytes; the 16-byte size is likely a multiple of the true unit.
- C-1803–C-1809: reserved.
