## Question

What frames does the SH-4 receive from the VCAN microcontroller over `UAT3:`,
and which vehicle signals can be attributed to a named consumer with a VA?

## Hypothesis

`CAN.dll` is a stream driver that opens a UART and receives *decoded* CAN
frames (id + 8 bytes), not raw CAN wire bytes; the five named signals exist as
setters with VAs. **Falsifier (task):** signal names exist only as strings and
no decode function references them. **Second falsifier:** a checksum routine
found in `CAN.dll` would mean the wire framing is recoverable statically and
the "UNKNOWN" conclusion is wrong.

## Interpretation

- **FACT:** `CAN.dll` owns `UAT3:` and exposes `CAN1:`; the SH-4 receives
  decoded CAN objects (`can_id` + 8 bytes), not raw CAN wire bits (O1–O6).
- **FACT:** default 230 400 bps, variable-length requests and named command
  classes exist (O4, O8).
- **FACT:** all five requested signals are named with a VA and a consumer or
  probe label; only speed has a verified scale; reverse has no decoder
  (O9, O11) — the task's first falsifier did not fire.
- **OBSERVED:** 60-byte application record and the 17-frame subscription
  (O7, O10).
- **UNKNOWN:** wire framing, checksum, endian, scheduling, byte offsets and
  runtime baud selection (O11, O12).
- **HYPOTHESIS:** none promoted beyond what the commands show; the note that
  `GPI1: IOCTL 0x80092064` may reset the sub-CPU stays a hypothesis.

## Conclusion

`docs/firmware/can.md` now separates the application layer from the `UAT3:`
wire, documents the decoded frame layout with offsets and VAs, lists the 17
frames and the five requested signals with a VA each, and states explicitly
that the wire grammar is unknown with two negative controls. Ten claim rows
C-7460–C-7469 were added. A byte-exact peer cannot be built from this record;
M8.H50 must label its encoding as a model and M8.H51 needs an L4 trace before
claiming guest acceptance.
