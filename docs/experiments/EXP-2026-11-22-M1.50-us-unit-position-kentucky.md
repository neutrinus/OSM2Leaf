## Question

Marek's US 2011 unit (map card QSD-301-103) is in Poland. The map screen shows
the car "somewhere in the US", and OpenCARWINGS reports 37.4058805556,
−84.4876444444 (between Somerset and Richmond, Kentucky), *At Home: No*, last
updated 2026-11-22 11:15. Is it the GPS receiver (region lock), or does the
WinCE side hold or replace the position? Four sub-questions: (1) where the
position comes from; (2) is the coordinate a constant in the firmware;
(3) who sends the position to OpenCARWINGS; (4) relation to the area code
(M2.7) and the SD lock.

| F6 | **The service mode can write a position and a date into the receiver.** `TestMode.exe` (ROM C) `fn 0x0003aa80` = `CTestModeGPSInit::WriteGpsData`: calls `fn 0x00043da8` (`GpsDataSetDate`), `Sleep`, then `fn 0x00043f70` (`GpsDataSetLatLon`). `0x00043f70` parses an `N/S` and an `E/W` text field of its object (+0x8c, +0x94), packs lat and lon as three 7-bit bytes each into a frame `0xA0, 0x00, lat×3, lon×3, 0, 0, sum&0x7f, 0xF0`. ### Hypotheses (not settled here)

| # | Hypothesis | For | Against / how to test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | **Most likely: the receiver has no current fix, and the unit shows and sends the last stored position.** The car was last positioned in Kentucky (previous owner, before export); Navi keeps the last car position across power cycles (M8.H19) and Vehicle.exe passes `OBJ_CarPos` to the TCU with `valid = 0`, which OpenCARWINGS shows as current (F3). | F1–F3, F5 (`LAST` status), F8 (not a default, so it is a remembered position) | If true, the diagnostic GPS page shows 0 satellites used / position invalid; the OpenCARWINGS raw packet has bit 7 of the flag byte = 0. If the point moves a little after driving (dead reckoning from Kentucky), H1 is confirmed too. |
| H1a | Why no fix: **GPS week-number rollover** in a 2010-era receiver (April 2019 or a firmware-specific later date) gives a wrong date; with a wrong date and a seeded position 8 000 km away the almanac predicts the wrong satellites and the receiver may never converge. | the receiver relies on stored almanac/time (F5) and accepts a date from the host (F6) | `GPS GMT=` on the diagnosis page: a date off by 1024 weeks (~19.6 years) settles it. |
| H1b | Why no fix: antenna/cable fault (car imported, roof antenna or connector damaged). | `GPS_ANT` status exists (F6) | TestMode `GPS_ANT` ≠ `CONNECTED`, or 0 satellites *searched*. |
| H2 | The receiver has a fix in Poland, but `Navi.exe` rejects it (position jump filter, off-map check) and keeps the dead-reckoned position in Kentucky. | `navdrv` has `SetFilterConfig` (F4); a map-matched car position is not the raw fix | Diagnosis page shows a valid fix and 4+ satellites while the map stays in the US. Static check: follow-up task M1.51. |
| H3 | Region lock in the receiver. | — | GPS receivers do not lock to a region; nothing in the firmware ties the position to the area code (F9). Unlikely. |
| H4 | The SD lock (M2.13) plays a role. | — | The lock only gates card access with CMD42; the card is unlocked in this car (it boots and shows maps). No relation. |

### Revised verdict

- **H1 (no fix, held position) is falsified** by U1: the receiver works and
  knows it is in Wrocław.
- **H3 (receiver region lock) is falsified** by U1.
- **The block is in the WinCE navigation (`Navi.exe` car-position logic), not in
  the receiver**: the raw fix is right (U1), the navigation's car position
  (`OBJ_CarPos`, which `Vehicle.exe` sends to the TCU → OpenCARWINGS, F1) stays
  in Kentucky (U3). This is H2, now the main hypothesis. Candidate reasons, not
  yet separated:
  - **H2a — date:** fixes are timestamped 2007, older than the stored
    position/history (2011–2019 dates); a "fix older than the last known state"
    check would drop every fix. The rollover is a fact (U2); its effect on
    `Navi.exe` is a hypothesis.
  - **H2b — distance:** a fix 8 000 km from the dead-reckoned position is
    treated as a GPS error (jump filter), forever.
  - **H2c — map:** the US card has no data at Wrocław, so map matching cannot
    place the car and keeps the last matched position.
- The rollover also explains a wrong date in trip/probe records and in
  anything that uses GPS time.
