## Question

For a synthetic street grid with deterministic curvature, how does Douglas–Peucker
tolerance (1–20 m at level 1, scaled by the parcel-width ratio above it) trade
compressed parcel-record bytes and kept vertices against maximum geometric
deviation — at what tolerance does a parcel stop overflowing its fixed record
slot, and what tolerance should the compiler profile ship with?

## Hypothesis

Compressed bytes are non-increasing in tolerance at every road level, so an
overflow point exists; a small tolerance just above that point keeps most of the
curvature at a fraction of the unsimplified byte cost. Falsifier: bytes do **not**
fall as tolerance rises (cost is elsewhere — then record where), or an
over-budget parcel passes the overflow check.

## Plan

1. **Baseline.** Generate the seed-1 town; read it with `tilewriter.load_imf`.
   Its ways are 2-point segments, so Douglas–Peucker alone cannot remove an
   interior vertex. The measurement tool therefore densifies every segment
   deterministically (25 m spacing, 12 m sine wiggle) to make the "street grid
   with curves" the task asks for. No network, no vendor geometry, no edit to
   `tools/osm_synth.py`.
2. **Sweep.** For levels 1 and 2 (the only levels `tilewriter.PARCEL_DEG` maps
   to road parcels; levels 3–5 are land/coast levels with degree-based simplify)
   and base tolerances `0, 0.5, 1, 2, 2.5, 3, 5, 8, 12, 20` m, simplify each way,
   build each populated parcel's real `0x00fa` record with
   `tilewriter.build_road_record`, and count compressed bytes, kept vertices,
   maximum removed-vertex deviation, and parcels above the budget. Level 2 uses
   `scale = parcel_width(2)/parcel_width(1)`, computed from
   `clarion_bg.parcel_geo` (expected 4), applied to the same base tolerances.
3. **Budget.** Calibrate a fixed per-parcel record budget from the factory tile's
   road-record block lengths in the town's directory `(84,940)` (one-off
   calibration, printed by the tool; Q1 is the candidate). Record the full
   distribution, not just the chosen number.
4. **Defaults.** Pick the smallest tested level-1 tolerance with zero overflow at
   both levels under that budget, rounded up for margin, and write it into
   `profiles/dolnoslaskie-test.toml` together with the proportional level-2
   value and the budget.
5. **Guard.** The tool exits 1 under `--check` when any parcel exceeds the
   profile budget; the selftest contains the positive and negative controls.
6. **Record.** Quote the tool's printed table and check results verbatim in
   Observations; keep Interpretation separate.

3. **Controls.** *Positive*: `python3 tools/tilewriter.py --selftest` and
   `python3 tools/slot_budget.py --selftest` pass; the `--tolerances 3 --check`
   run exits 0. *Negative*: `--tolerances 0 --check` exits 1 on the dense town;
   the selftest's synthetic over-budget row raises `FitError`. *Falsifier*:
   total compressed bytes must be non-increasing in tolerance at each level;
   if any step rises, `check_monotonic` fails with exit 1 and the EXP records
   where the cost sits.
4. **Scope.** One generated town (125 nodes, 56 ways, 2 relations in JSONL;
   54 drivable road ways after `load_imf`), one synthetic curvature transform
   (25 m / 12 m), 10 base tolerances × 2 road levels = 20 sweep rows, one factory
   directory's 252 even-pair record blocks for budget calibration. No firmware
   code, no L3/L4/L5 claim, no vendor data in the repo.
5. **Falsifier.** A rising byte count as tolerance rises; a dense (0 m) parcel
   that passes `--check`; or a level-2 scale other than the parcel-width ratio
   derived from `clarion_bg.parcel_geo`.

## Interpretation

- **FACT:** The tool reproduces the requested table in one command; all 20 rows were printed by `tools/slot_budget.py geom`, and bytes are non-increasing in tolerance at both levels (`monotonic: PASS`). The falsifier did not fire.
- **FACT:** The budget calibration is a distribution, not a guess: 252 factory road blocks, Q1 372 B, median 982 B. 372 B is used as a *design target* for generated records (small enough to keep an in-place slot), not claimed as a firmware limit.
- **FACT:** The first tested zero-overflow tolerance is 2.5 m at level 1 and 4 m actual at level 2; the committed 3 m / 12 m defaults leave 42 B and 158 B of headroom respectively at the largest parcel.
- **OBSERVED:** The seed-1 town has only 2-point ways; without the tool's deterministic densification the sweep would be a flat line. The 25 m / 12 m transform and the budget are both committed as profile parameters so the table is reproducible.
- **OBSERVED:** The gate's selftest step exceeded its 60 s per-tool timeout on two unrelated tools; both pass with a 180 s timeout, so the result is host load, not a broken tool.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** Other seeds and the real Dolnośląskie extract will have different overflow points but the same ordering. Not measured here (M5.77).
- **UNKNOWN:** Absolute byte counts on stock zlib 1.3.1 (this host has zlib-ng), and firmware acceptance (rule 13) — no L3/L4/L5 run was made.

## Conclusion

`tools/slot_budget.py` gains a `geom` sweep and `geom-stats` calibration subcommand plus a 13-check geometry-budget selftest section; `profiles/dolnoslaskie-test.toml` ships the measured defaults (`simplify_metres = 3`, `l2_simplify_metres = 12`, `max_record_bytes = 372`, densify 25 m / 12 m). The one-command table, the positive/negative budget controls and the monotonicity guard are recorded above; claims C-7940–C-7949 back them. No `docs/format/` change was needed — the tool uses the already-documented `0x00fa` record layout. Still open: stock-zlib re-measurement, the real-extract sweep (M5.77), and firmware acceptance.
