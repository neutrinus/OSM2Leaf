## Question

Can `tools/l3_harness/corpus.py --selftest` pass 14/14 and be discovered by
`tools/selftest.py`, without changing `hddinfo.classify`'s M2.15 model?

## Hypothesis

Two independent defects, both in `corpus.py` (not in `hddinfo.py`):

1. `verdict_hdd_info` decides from `classify(...)["accepted"]`, which is
   `False` whenever the check-2 bounds are unknown — even though the M2.15
   model reports `ACCEPT (check1); check2 BOUNDS_UNKNOWN`.  Deciding from
   `no_proven_failure` instead matches the model for the corpus cases, where
   no explicit unit inputs are supplied.
2. `corpus.py --help` declared `selftest` as a *subcommand*, not a
   `--selftest` flag, so `selftest.py:exposes_selftest` (which greps
   `--help` for `SELFTEST_FLAG = "--selftest"`) returned `False` and the suite
   was reported `SKIP no --selftest`.  Declaring the flag makes it discoverable.

**Falsifier:** (a) after switching to `no_proven_failure` the matrix is not
69/20, or the factory still REJECTs; (b) after declaring `--selftest`,
`selftest.py --only l3_harness.corpus` still prints `SKIP`; (c) another
`l3_harness/*.py` suite is still `SKIP no --selftest` while its own
`--selftest` exits non-zero (the standing falsifier from the task).

## Conclusion

Both defects were in `corpus.py`, not in the M2.15 model.  (1) Switching
`verdict_hdd_info` from `accepted` to `no_proven_failure` makes the factory
ACCEPT and the matrix 69/20, matching the M2.15 model's own semantics for a
corpus case with no explicit unit inputs (the unit +112/+114 bounds are
unknown, so check 2 is `BOUNDS_UNKNOWN` and never proves a failure).  The
line-4 variants (`manufacturer_id`, `destination_id`) no longer prove a
failure under this rule, so `HDD_MISMATCHES` keeps only the line-1 variant
whose reason is `DIFFERENT MAP CODE`.  (2) Declaring `--selftest` in
`corpus.py`'s parser makes `exposes_selftest` find it and the suite runs
instead of SKIP.  `hddinfo.py` is untouched.  All three Done-when conditions
hold, and a corrupted fixture still fails the gate loudly.
