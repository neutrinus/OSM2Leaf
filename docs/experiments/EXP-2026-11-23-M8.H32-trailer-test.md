# EXP-2026-11-23-M8.H32-trailer-test: wheel pulses on `-M ze0`, and a trailer move from Kentucky to Chicago

- **Status:** done. The speed input works: the firmware's speed variable
  follows the pulse rate (50 km/h within 1 %, 0 at 0). The first trailer
  runs (O6–O10) failed: the locator jumped to the GPS position once and
  the next cycle put the car back. The cause was the GPS peer, not the
  firmware (O11–O13): its reports did not agree with each other. With
  position, speed and heading that agree, the car moves from Kentucky to
  Chicago and the map follows the GPS.

## Question

M8.H18 left the car in Kentucky. A US fix was held back by bit 0x80, the
vehicle's own speed, and by bits 0x01 and 0x02. Can the emulator turn the
wheels? With wheels turning and 15+ good fixes at a far US place (the
trailer case), does the car move there?

## Hypothesis

The firmware's DR sample thread waits on an interrupt the emulator never
raises; modelling wheel pulses and the gyro makes the speed input live. With
wheels turning and 15+ self-consistent GPS fixes at a far US place, the
locator's five-sample gate passes and the car follows the GPS.

   printf 'fix 41.8781 -87.75\nspeed 30\nheading 45\ndrive on\non\n' | socat - UNIX-CONNECT:$W/s22/gps.ctl
   printf 'qom-set /machine/dr pulses-milli 5000\n' | socat - UNIX-CONNECT:$W/s22/mon.sock
   python3 tools/ze0_screenshot.py screen --monitor $W/s22/mon.sock -o $W/s22/shot.png

   The original steps 1-4 (static getter walk, the QEMU DR model, the S20 run
   with one input changed at a time) are the run recipe; every printed value is
   quoted in O1-O17.

3. **Controls.** Positive: with `pulses-milli 5000` and GPS 30 km/h the speed
   word `*0x00dbdc2e` is 300 and bit 0x80 is set (O4), and with agreeing GPS
   reports the car moves from Kentucky to Chicago (O13). Negative: 0 pulses
   give speed 0; the old peer reports made computed speed 5-8x too low and the
   car alternated (O6-O12). A standing car with a good fix takes the position
   at once (O15). Falsifier ("speed does not change with pulse rate") does not

4. **Scope.** NK1 + US 2011 card on the M8.H18 NOR flash model; the DR sensor

   gyro values other than the table in O17, reverse, a shutdown save (O16),
   US build other than this card, car/bench (L5). Screens stay outside the repo.

5. **Falsifier.** The speed variable does not change with the pulse rate, or a
   consistent far US fix does not move the car. O4 and O13 show both changes,
   so the falsifier did not fire.

## Observations

O1 (the wait). The sample thread `0x039e55b0` calls `InterruptInitialize`
(coredll #627 through stub `0x039e6efc`) with SYSINTR 46. It then loops:
wait, `0x039e5194` (sample, via the builder `0x039e59e4`), `0x039e5290`
(record ring), `0x039e74bc` (writes 1 to the status-clear register),
`InterruptDone(46)`. In the guest's NK1 OAL table at `0x88005bc8`, row 20 is
{INTEVT 0x760, ISR `0x8803f004`, IntrPrio 11, SYSINTR 0x2e}. That ISR writes
0x00400000 to `INT2MSKR` (`0xff804040`), so INT2 bit 22, and returns 46. QEMU
had no source for 0x760, so the thread never woke. `gyroAdc_GetAddData` hits
seen in S15 come from another caller (`0x03cf636c` on NK1).

O2 (registers). The pointer tables give the offsets of C-7291. The trace of
S20 shows the driver's init writes: gyro +0x00 = 1, +0x08 = 0xd2, +0x0c =
0xcd14, +0x04 = 1; speed +0x04 = 0xd2, +0x1c = 0x54, +0x08 = 1, then 0. After
that, every tick reads gyro +0x30…+0x3c and +0x40, speed +0x0c, +0x10,
+0x104 and +0x110, then writes speed +0x18 = 1. All of it goes through the
area-7 aliases. A model at the P4 addresses alone saw no access (S19).

O3 (model). `ze0-h32-dr-sensors.patch` adds INT2 source `DR` (0x760, bit 22,
level 11) and the object `/machine/dr`:

- +0x0c returns a pulse counter latched each tick; the thread takes the u16
  difference (`0x039e5634`–`0x039e5650`);
- +0x10 returns the tick count;
- the line goes up every `tick-ms` (100) of virtual time once the driver has
  written the latch enable, and down on the status-clear write.

Raising it from the first tick (S18) held the CPU in the interrupt before
the driver was ready: in 7 minutes only 15 s of guest time passed. With the
model, `0x0049c7f0` (message 0x04c1 subcode 0x131, dispatched by
`0x00498fbc`) runs about once a second.

O4 (speed). `*0x00dbdc2e`, read at `0x0049ca7c`:

| pulses/s | GPS | value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | moving, 30 km/h | 0 |
| 30 | none yet, right after boot | 209 |
| 30, 50, 60 | moving, 30 km/h | 300 |
| 60 | fix, speed 0 | 360 |
| 30 | fix, speed 0 | 180 |
| 30 | no fix | 180 |
| 83.33 | no fix | 498, 504, 498 |

The unit is 0.1 km/h. Without GPS speed the value is linear in the pulse
rate, with a scale the firmware learned while the GPS was moving (209 →
180 for the same rate). 50 km/h comes out within 1 %, and no pulses give 0.
With pulses on, bit 0x80 (`+22 ≥ 100`, `0x0049eeea`) is set.

O5 (stat byte). With pulses and stat 0x30 the flags word reads 0x1fc: bits
0x01 and 0x02 are missing. `0x0049dba0` (bit 0x02) clears the bit unless the
first halfword of each of the last 4 GPS history entries is 0x00 or 0x10.
`0x0049da38` (bit 0x01) weighs the last 15 entries differently for 0x00/0x10
and 0x20/0x30. After `stat 00` the word went 0x1fc → 0x1fe → 0x1ff → 0x3ff.
The peer's default is now 0x00.

O6 (the jump and the way back). With 0x3ff, `0x0049fa44` writes the GPS
position `0x9f62a223`, which decodes to (41.84, −87.77), to `0x00dbce00` and
calls the direction init `0x004aa070`. In the next cycle `0x0049e08c` looks
the car mesh up in the table at `0x00dd48e8`, gets −1 (the table holds 1
mesh: pointer `0x00dbede0`, count 1, and the Kentucky mesh), and
`0x0049e164` writes back `0x9c03a3c0`. `OBJ_CarPos_write` alternates
between the two words (15 and 15 in 40 s). The flags word alternates
between 0x3ff and 0x0ff. After 5+ minutes of driving the screen still showed
Simpson Rd with the car mark, the same picture as before.

O7 (why it goes back). The mesh table is filled only after a successful
map-matching cycle (`0x00456cc8` → `0x00451f8c`, EXP M8.H78 O4). Here it
returns 0xffff each time (`@(36,r15)` at `0x00479dea`), and the current
road link `*0x00dbdc30` stays `0x00dc9ac0`. Its exit at `0x00458990`
follows a counter, `*0x00dbd5ac` (`0x004588f0`–`0x0045897a`). The counter goes up
when the match state `*0x00dd48d4` is 1, goes down when it is −1, and at 8
or more calls `0x00454be8(3)` and `0x00478e18(2)`. That is a partial reset
which keeps the road link (O9). In S20 the state was 0 and the counter 0 on
every hit.

O8 (the match state). `*0x00dd48d4` is written only in `0x00456098`
(`0x00456814`–`0x004568dc`, called from `0x00456cc8`). After 30 samples
(counter `*0x00dca7bc`) it compares two speed sums, `*0x00dd48e0` against
the reference in `@(32,r15)`, as a percentage. Outside 81–119 % it sets the
lost-road counter `*0x00dbd5ac` straight to 8. Inside 96–104 % it writes −1,
and otherwise +1. In S20 with 50 pulses/s and GPS 30 km/h the ratio was 100
and the state −1. With 100 pulses/s (wheels twice as fast as the GPS) the
ratio fell to 91, 90, 89, the counter reached 8, and `0x00478e18(2)` ran
(called from `0x0045897c`, and with 4 from `0x004562da`). So the counter
measures whether the wheel speed agrees with the GPS speed, not whether the
GPS position agrees with the road. A trailer move followed by normal driving
keeps the two speeds in agreement.

O9 (what a reset clears). `0x00478e18` with 2 or 4 skips the first block
(`0x00478e2c`–`0x00478e38` branch to `0x00479288`) and leaves the road link
`*0x00dbdc30` in place. In S20 it stayed `0x00dc9ac0` after the mismatch
reset. Only arguments 0, 1 and 3 clear it. Those come from the positioning
message dispatcher `0x00452738`: subcode 110 (`0x478e18(1)`, then
`0x00477e98`) and subcode 116 (`0x478e18(3)` or `(1)` by the parameter at
+12). They also come from `0x00452968` (0, 1, 2). The senders of those
subcodes were not found: the subcode is a data field, not an immediate
next to `0x0fa0`.

O10 (forcing a reset). A gdb hook at `0x00452758` rewrote one 0x66
message into subcode 116 with parameter 1. The road link became 0 and
stayed 0. In another try it was rewritten three times into 110 with
parameter 0. Either way the car word kept alternating between Chicago
(`0x9f70a223`, a little further north than in O6) and Kentucky, and the flags between
0x3ff and 0x0ff. With no road link, `0x00456cc8` exits at once (EXP M8.H78
O5), so the mesh table is never refilled. The reset branch `0x0049e164`
keeps restoring the saved position from `0x00def7c0`/`0x00dc0408`/
`0x00dbd104`.

Not tested: gyro values other than 0 (the scale and zero of
`gyroAdc_GetAddData` are unknown), reverse, a drive long enough for the
counter to move, and a matched start on a road near the new place.

### O11. The real "use the GPS position" decision (static + breakpoints)

Map-matching `0x00456cc8` has a third argument, an out word that starts at
-1. Eight places set it to 0 (`0x00458394` … `0x004586ec`); only then does
the code at `0x0045898a` continue to `0x00458994`, the branch with the debug
texts `GPS Pos use (date)` and `pos_jg condition = [%d], dir_use = %d`.
The places form a ladder on the GPS-to-car distance L (frame +72): L < 100
gives condition 1, L < 200 condition 11, and so on; L >= 700 is condition
15 at `0x004586dc`. Condition 15 passes only if `*0x00dd4a2e` is 0 and a
fail mask has neither 0x1000 nor 0x4000.

The fail mask is built at `0x00457bc8`–`0x00457e34` from a flags word that
the callee `0x00455210` fills: flag 0x100 gives mask 0x1000. In the failing
runs (breakpoint at `0x004586fc`) the condition was 15, L was 10000 (the
cap) and the mask was 0x1000 on every cycle; `0x00458994` was never
reached.

### O12. Flag 0x100 is "the GPS track is not self-consistent yet"

`0x00455210` (code `0x00455e64`–`0x00455f2a`) sets flag 0x100 when the
distance is >= 1000 and the latch `*0x00dca8f2` is 0. The latch is set once
two counters, `*0x00dbdc36` and `*0x00dbd0da`, both reach 5. They count up
(to 60) once per GPS sample while the reported GPS speed is >= 100
(10 km/h); each drops back to 0 on a sample that fails its test
(`0x00455d46`–`0x00455dea`):

- speed test (`0x00455982`–`0x00455a96`, counter `*0x00dbdc36`): the
  distance between two consecutive GPS positions, times 36, divided by the
  sample count, must lie between the smaller reported speed minus 50 and
  the larger plus 50 (0.1 km/h). The firmware takes consecutive reports as
  one second apart.
- heading test (`0x00455bae`–`0x00455d44`, counter `*0x00dbd0da`): the
  bearing between the two positions must lie within the reported headings
  +/- 15 degrees (150; a wider window at low speed).

Measured at `0x00455a4a` and `0x00455d46` with the old driving script:
reported speed 300, computed speed 36; reported heading 0 or 90, computed
bearing 900 or 0. Both counters stayed 0.

Two faults of the peer side explain it. `gps.py` sent an extra report on
every control line, so the script's five position updates per second made
five reports per second, each 1/5 of a second of travel: the computed
speed was 5 to 8 times too low. And the bearing the firmware computed was 90 degrees for a drive
north and 0 for a drive east, while the peer reported 0 and 90 (compass).
The firmware compares the two numbers directly, so the receiver's heading
field most likely counts from east, counter-clockwise; 45 degrees is the
same in both, and is what the passing run used. Not settled: see Next.

### O13. Consistent reports: the car follows the GPS

`gps.py` got a `drive on` control: every report moves the fix by speed x
period along the heading, and control lines no longer add reports while
driving. Run S22, fresh boot, car in Kentucky; then `fix 41.8781 -87.75`,
`speed 30`, `heading 45`, `drive on`, `on`, and `pulses-milli 5000`:

| time after first fix | event |
| --- | --- |
| 7–10 s | `0x00453538` runs each sample; flags `*0x00dbd8ac` go 0xac → 0x1be |
| 11 s | `0x00458994` reached ("GPS Pos use"), then `0x00478e18(1)` twice (called from `0x00479f2c`): the old road link is cleared |
| 13 s | `0x0049e164` runs once (the saved-position reset); road link changes |
| 36 s | `0x00458994` again; flags 0x3ff; link `0x00dc9a20` |
| after | car mesh `*0x00dbdce0` = `0x9f62a220` on every read; `0x0049e164` not hit again |

The screen shows Chicago (W Washington Blvd, Austin) with the car arrow,
and a second screenshot 40 s later is shifted north-east, as driven. In
run S21 (same inputs, on a unit that had been alternating as in O6) the
counters were seen at 0x20–0x22, the latch at 1, the fail mask 0 and the
condition 1 with L between 25 and 53. After `speed 0` and `pulses-milli 0`
the car stayed in Chicago (flags 0x7f).

`0x00477e98` and `0x00489a74` ("Make Cand at GPS pos") were not called
during the takeover (0 hits in 150 s), and no subcode 110/116 message is
involved. Gyro stayed 0 the whole time; the serial log repeats
`LOC_SMEVENT305 rcv error(Gyro)`.

### O14. Heading field of the report: 0 = east, counter-clockwise

Run S22, car following the fix, `drive on` at 30 km/h. Breakpoint
`0x00455d46`: r7 is the bearing computed from two positions, r4 the upper
bound from the reported heading (+150).

| driven (compass) | reported field | computed bearing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 (north) | 0 | 900 |
| 90 (east) | 900 | 0 |
| 180 (south) | 1800 | 2700 |
| 270 (west) | 2700 | 1800 |

The heading counter `*0x00dbd0da` stayed 0 in all four. The computed
bearing is (90 - compass) mod 360. `gps.py encode_report` now converts
compass degrees to that field; with it a drive north gives 900 against
900 and a drive west 1800 against 1800, and both counters count (10 and
22 at the time of reading).

### O15. A standing car takes the GPS position at once, without wheels

Run S23: fresh boot (car in Kentucky), `pulses-milli 0`, then
`fix 41.8781 -87.75`, `speed 0`, `on`. Within 45 s `0x00458994` was reached
once, the car mesh was `0x9f62a220` and the screen showed Chicago. At
`0x00455e64` the two words that gate flag 0x100 read `*0x00dbcd60` = 1
and `*0x00dbdbc0` = 0; in the failing moving runs (O11) they were 0 and 1.
So flag 0x100, and with it the five-sample test of O12, applies only to a
moving car. The first trailer runs failed because the wheels turned while
the fix stood still.

### O16. No flash write while driving or standing

S23 ran with `--flash-rw` on a copy of the dump. After the takeover, a
drive, a stop and about 12 minutes of guest time, the copy was still
byte-identical to the dump (`cmp`: 0 differences). `docs/firmware/power.md`
lists the commit steps in the shutdown path after ACC off (SRM commit,
FMD write disable). ACC off comes from the VCAN sub-micro, whose peer
stops the NK1 boot today (M8.H75), so the save could not be triggered.
When the position is saved is therefore still open; this run only shows
that it is not saved continuously.

### O17. Gyro: channel 0, 16 bits, must be non-zero

The handler `0x0049c7f0` prints `LOC_SMEVENT305 rcv error(Gyro)` at
`0x0049cc3e` when the sample's gyro field (message +28) is 0. With
`gyro0` = 0x100 the field read 0x100, `*0x00df0ef2` became 1 and the error
stopped; `gyro1`…`gyro7` (set to other values) did not show up. 0x10000 and
0x100000 read as 0 again: the field is 16 bits wide.

With the GPS off and the wheels turning, the handler's heading step per
message (r4 at `0x0049cc50`, 0.1 degree, heading in `*0x00dd4888`):

| `gyro0` | step per message |
| --- | --- |
| 0x2000 | -20 |
| 0x4000 | -60 |
| 0x7c00 | -135.3 |
| 0x8000 | -140.3 |
| 0x8400 | -145.3 |

That is linear: -0.00488 per count, zero crossing near 0x1000. Over 300
ticks (30 s) at 0x3000 the heading fell by 118.7 degrees, so messages
come once a second and one count is about 0.0005 degree/s. A larger word
turns the heading number down, which is clockwise in the convention of
O14 (a right turn). Close to the zero crossing the step drifts by itself
(0xf00: +48 to +56 in ten messages; 0x1100: -10 to -21), and 0x100 and
0x800 gave +19 and +10: the unit adapts its gyro bias, so the zero is a
learned value, not a constant. The real sensor's rest value is not known
from this.

Not tested: reverse, the save at shutdown (O16).

## Interpretation

The emulator now has wheels. `navdrv` gets its 100 ms interrupt, the samples
reach `Navi.exe`, and the speed variable follows the pulse rate (M8.H32
done-when: 50 km/h within 5 %, 0 at 0, C-7294).

The trailer move works when the GPS reports agree with each other. The
locator trusts a GPS position that is more than 1 km from the car only
after five consecutive samples, at more than 10 km/h, in which the
reported speed and heading match the speed and bearing computed from the
positions (C-8830–C-8832). Then map-matching takes the "GPS Pos use"
branch, drops the old road link, and the car stays at the new place
(C-8833). The earlier negative result (O6–O10, C-7297–C-7299) came from
the peer: extra reports made the computed speed several times too low
(C-8834). The lost-road counter and subcodes 110/116 are not part of this
path.

A standing car is the easy case: with a good fix it takes the GPS
position at once, wheels or not (O15, C-8836). So for a real car after a
trailer move, standing with a good fix should be enough, and the
five-sample test only matters while driving (PROBABLE, not tested on the
unit). The Poland case of C-8647 is unchanged: there the fix fails
earlier, at the mesh conversion.

Falsifier ("the speed variable does not change with the pulse rate"): does
not fire (O4).

## Conclusion

The emulator now has wheels: the DR sample thread gets its 100 ms interrupt and
the speed variable follows the pulse rate (50 km/h within 1 %, 0 at 0;
C-7294). The trailer move works when the GPS reports agree with each other;
then map-matching takes the "GPS Pos use" branch and the car stays in Chicago
(C-8830-C-8833). The earlier negative (O6-O10) came from the peer sending
inconsistent speed/heading (C-8834). A standing car takes a good fix at once
(O15). The falsifier does not fire.
