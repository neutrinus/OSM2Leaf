## Question

Can one standard-library tool take a validated IMF v1 file and produce a file
in which **every way lies in exactly one `BxxRyyR` cell**, with the exact
border intersection points as nodes, stable new ids, preserved tags and
resolvable relations — and with `tools/imf_check.py --assign-tiles` a
byte-identical no-op on the result?

## Hypothesis

The cell grid is known (`lat_min = int(B,16)*2/3`, `lon_max = 108-int(R,16)`,
8 × 8 grid units), so the borders are closed-form lines:

```
lat = 16/3 * k          # multiples of 8 grid units of 2/3 deg
lon = 4 + 8 * k         # columns are 4 mod 8: ..., 4, 12, 20, 28, ...
```

A segment between two vertices in different cells therefore crosses a known,
finite set of lines, and the intersections can be computed exactly (one axis
snapped to the border, the other interpolated).  Expected snag: IMF cells are
half-open, so a point *on* a border belongs to the cell east/north of it; a
single node cannot then be the last node of the west fragment and the first
node of the east fragment without one of them listing two cells in `tiles`
(`E012`/`E013`).  The hypothesis is that this is the only real difficulty and
that it is solved by giving each side its own node.

## Interpretation

- **FACT (C-1040):** `tools/mesh_split.py` splits an IMF v1 file into ways
  that each lie in exactly one cell.  On the synthetic fixture (double
  crossing, roundabout, border vertex, border-following edge, latitude
  border, restriction) 6 ways become 11 fragments, 4 of them cross-border,
  with 9 new nodes; the result passes `tools/imf_check.py` with 0 errors and
  0 warnings, every way's `tiles` has exactly one entry, and
  `tools/imf_check.py --assign-tiles` reproduces the file byte-identically
  (`cmp` silent).  Re-running the splitter on its own output is a byte-level
  fixpoint, and two runs on one input are byte-identical.
- **FACT (C-1041):** the borders are `lat = 16/3 * k` and `lon = 4 + 8 * k`
  (columns of the `BxxRyyR` grid are `4 mod 8`, not multiples of 8).  The
  intersection is exact: the crossed axis is set to the border value, the
  other is the linear interpolation at the same parameter, and a corner
  crossing is merged into one point.  The two crossings of the fixture's
  `w100` are `(51.150000000000006, 20.0)` and `(51.25, 20.0)`, and the
  latitude crossing is exactly `lat_border(10) = 53.33333333333333`.
- **FACT (C-1042):** half-open cells make a shared border node impossible.
  `imf_check` derives a way's `tiles` from its nodes' cells (`E013`) and a
  node exactly on a border belongs to the cell east/north of it, so one node
  at the crossing would force one fragment to list two cells.  The splitter
  therefore gives the owning (east/north) fragment the exact intersection
  point and the other fragment a copy pressed at most `1e-7` deg (about 1 cm)
  inside its cell.  Both fragments reach the border and stay single-cell; the
  writer joins them (M5.5) through those two near-identical endpoints
  (`n9000000000001` at 19.99999998 vs `n9000000000002` at 20.0).
- **FACT (C-1043):** the id the task suggested, `w<osm>.<cell>.<n>`, is not
  legal IMF: `imf_check` `E007` accepts only `[nwr]` followed by digits, and
  the self-test pins that a dotted id is rejected.  The splitter encodes the
  same information numerically: `w<osm><BB><RRR><nn>` (cell row and column in
  hex, then the fragment index), e.g. `w1004805001` = piece 01 of way 100 in
  `B48R050R`.  A way that needs no split keeps its original id, so unsplit
  ways and their relation members are untouched.
- **OBSERVED:** the exact border values of the latitude borders are
  `16/3 * k`, not representable in the 7 decimals that
  `intermediate-format.md` promises for coordinates (53.33333333333333 has
  14).  `imf_check` does not enforce the decimal count, and rounding to 7
  decimals would move the point south of the border and change its cell, so
  the splitter keeps full precision on border nodes only.
- **OBSERVED:** one segment can cross several borders at once.  A 48.1 °N /
  12.1 °E → 58.0 °N / 35.0 °E segment yields four fragments
  (`B48R058R`, `B48R050R`, `B50R050R`, `B50R048R`), including two cells that
  contained no input vertex; each has its own id, one cell in `tiles`, and
  the file still passes `--assign-tiles` unchanged.
- **OBSERVED:** `--boundary-density` is implemented as a *spacing* value: the
  exact intersection point is always inserted (the double-crossing fixture
  cannot be split at all without it), and the flag additionally subdivides
  edges that run *on* a border into pieces of at most METRES (bare flag:
  500 m), inserting points exactly on the border.  Without that, a 3 km
  border-following road is one 3 km segment and the neighbouring cell cannot
  stitch to it.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** the same rules hold on a real Dolnośląskie extract.  Not
  tested: OSM hosts are blocked here (C-1716), so no `.pbf` was ingested and
  no regional file exists (M5.1b).  The fixture uses real grid arithmetic and
  real cell names, but synthetic geometry.
- **KNOWN LIMITATION (by design):** a `way` member of a relation is expanded
  to *all* its fragments, in travel order, with the original role.  For a
  turn restriction this keeps the relation valid (`E017`) but does not say
  which fragment is the near side of the `via` node; that is a writer/M5.5
  decision, and `INTER`/`FRWAY` cannot store restrictions yet anyway.
  Fragments dropped by `--min-piece-m` are removed from the members, and a
  restriction left without a `from`/`to`/`via` role is dropped and counted.

## Conclusion

`tools/mesh_split.py` completes the M5.2 scope: IMF v1 in, one way fragment
per `BxxRyyR` cell out, exact border intersections inserted, stable
digits-only ids, tags and derived fields preserved, relations kept valid, and
`tools/imf_check.py --assign-tiles` a byte-identical no-op on the result.
The one design constraint that surfaced is the half-open cell rule: exact
clipping costs two nodes per crossing, one per side, and the writer has to
join them.  `docs/research/intermediate-format.md` needs no edit for this
stage (the splitter is a post-IMF step, exactly as the document says: "what
the writer does at the page boundary is a writer decision"), but M5.4/M5.5
must consume the per-side pair rather than a shared node.
