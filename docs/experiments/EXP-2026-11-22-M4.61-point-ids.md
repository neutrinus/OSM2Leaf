# EXP-2026-11-22-M4.61: the `11xx` nibble is position in `COORD001`/`ADDRE`, not the POINT marker, and `ADDRE` has no fine position

- **Status:** done
- **Date:** 2026-11-22
- **Sample:** EU 2011 card `archive/sd-images/LEAF_2011-2012_QSD-309-101_navi158_eu.img`, SHA-256 `105e2032e480e8ba162a2a9d4b6624cb1dafee380379a9908f3f101b15076307`, `REFER001/COORD001.DAT` and the 33 `REFER001/ADDRE0nn.DAT` pairs (0.81 GiB decoded); US 2011 card `archive/sd-images/LEAF_2011-2012_US.img`, SHA-256 `53342430a1bb58d3ed6eb68c5563f079c710a400134d522e6192489b324c5383`, `COORD001` and the 11 `ADDRE` pairs (0.83 GiB decoded). No vendor bytes are committed.
- **Tools:** Python 3.13.18 / zlib 1.3 (stdlib, decode only); `tools/refer_id_census.py` (new, `--selftest`, 15 checks), `tools/coord.py` (`--selftest`, 22 checks), `tools/point_census.py` (`--selftest`, 33 checks), `tools/addre.py`, `tools/cmpind.py`, `tools/coord_zcode_icode.py`
- **Claims:** C-6560–C-6569

- **Superseded by:** none

## Question

Do `COORD001` bounding-box words and `ADDRE` coordinate ids use the POINT
copy-A form (low nibble `11xx` = two marker bits, then a 4-unit sub-cell that
two 11-bit fields refine), or are they plain 1-unit coordinates — and does an
`ADDRE` record carry the fine position?

## Hypothesis

The task inherits the question from C-2679 ("POINT copy-A words are not 1-unit
coordinates; their low nibble is `11xx`") and from `coord.py`, which decoded
`COORD001` and `ADDRE` with the plain `(x−32768)/192`, `(y−52742)/128` form.
Two readings are possible for an `11xx` nibble in an `ADDRE`/`COORD001` id:
(a) it is the same marker, so the id needs stripping; (b) it is four position
bits and the plain reading is correct. The hypothesis is **open**: a mixed
nibble census is a valid answer, and the filenames the task names may turn out
not to use the form at all.

**Falsifier:** a shard where the marker-stripped decode places its ids inside
that region's `COORD001` box while the plain decode does not (it would make
`11xx` a marker there); or an `ADDRE` record with a POINT-style
`(0x800|flon, flat)` pair at `id+6` that decodes inside the same 16-unit cell
as the plain coord (it would name a fine field).

## Method

1. **Sample and hash.** As above. The two `REFER001` trees are the decoded
   factory trees `/home/marek/projekty/leafre-work/m4.8b-eu/REFER001` (EU) and
   `/home/marek/projekty/leafre-work/m5.12-us/root-factory/REFER001` (US).
2. **Commands.**

   ```sh
   EU=/home/marek/projekty/leafre-work/m4.8b-eu/REFER001
   US=/home/marek/projekty/leafre-work/m5.12-us/root-factory/REFER001
   python3 tools/refer_id_census.py --selftest
   python3 tools/coord.py --selftest
   python3 tools/point_census.py --selftest
   # low-nibble census, every readable shard of both cards
   python3 tools/refer_id_census.py ids --eu "$EU" \
       --shards $(cd "$EU" && ls ADDRE*.DAT.cmp | sed 's/\.DAT\.cmp//')
   python3 tools/refer_id_census.py ids --us "$US" \
       --shards $(cd "$US" && ls ADDRE*.DAT.cmp | sed 's/\.DAT\.cmp//')
   # the decode control (plain vs marker-stripped, in the region's COORD001 box)
   python3 tools/refer_id_census.py ids --eu "$EU" --control \
       --shards ADDRE001 ADDRE014 ADDRE017 ADDRE032
   # the ADDRE fine-field search (every even offset of every record)
   python3 tools/refer_id_census.py fine --dir "$EU" \
       --shards $(cd "$EU" && ls ADDRE*.DAT.cmp | sed 's/\.DAT\.cmp//')
   python3 tools/refer_id_census.py fine --dir "$US" \
       --shards $(cd "$US" && ls ADDRE*.DAT.cmp | sed 's/\.DAT\.cmp//')
   # the precheck that gates the task
   python3 tools/point_census.py refs --us "$US" --eu "$EU" \
       --refs docs/experiments/EXP-2026-11-08-M4.8b-poi-refs.tsv
   ```

   `ids` decodes every shard (`addre.load_blob` -> `sections` ->
   `split_records` -> `parse_record`), reads the packed `coord` id, and
   reports the low-nibble histogram of both words per shard and per card;
   `COORD001` is read through `cmpind` + `coord_zcode_icode.coord001_records`
   (four coordinate words per 14-byte entry, no fine field). `--control` adds
   (i) `decode_control`: for each nibble class, the in-box rate with the plain
   id and with `coord.packed_units(word, 0)` (the marker stripped); and (ii)
   `box_control`: the plain ids against the plain box and against the
   marker-stripped box. `fine` scans every even offset of every whole address
   record for the POINT record pair `(0x800 | flon, flat)` (`fine_pair`), and
   for the `TT 40 XX YY` token at `id+6` (`point_token_at_id`).
3. **Controls.** *Positive*: the documented POINT record
   `6036a0cfd1bd0bda01f40a07a0c3d1b1` gives `fine_pair` at offset 6 =
   `(986, 500)` (`fine_pair: the documented POINT record pair at 6`) and the
   `--selftest` reproduces it; the `coord.py`/`point_census.py` selftests agree
   with each other on the same fixtures. *Negative*: the marker-stripped
   decode moves the ids out of the region box in every discriminating shard;
   the `TT 40 XX YY` token at `id+6` in a low-nibble synthetic record is
   rejected when the next word is not `0x40`; a "record" pair at `id+8` on EU
   `ADDRE001` decodes 2–6 km from the plain position (not a sub-cell).
4. **Scope.** All 33 EU + 11 US `ADDRE` shards and both `COORD001` files; 10 EU
   and 4 US shards stop `split_records` at a `0xc1` record (M4.9, open) and are
   named in the census, not counted. Excluded: firmware (M3.34 has the POINT
   reader), the `(XX,YY)` 2D frame (M4.19, C-1964), `REFER002`.
5. **Falsifier.** As in Hypothesis.

## Interpretation

- **OBSERVED (C-6560):** `COORD001` entries are 14 bytes with no fine field;
  all 132 (EU) / 44 (US) coordinate words carry the `11xx` low nibble. With
  the marker stripped, the box moves under every Monaco id (0/609 plain-box
  ids stay inside), so the box is read as plain 1-unit coordinates.
- **OBSERVED (C-6561):** `ADDRE` ids mix `11xx` and 0..7 nibbles, per shard and
  per card: EU `ADDRE001`/`ADDRE014` 100 % `11xx`, `ADDRE017` 114/1,218,
  `ADDRE032` 18/82; totals EU 16,539,516/17,409,252 and US
  32,719,161/32,981,740 words. The split is a property of the data, not noise
  (one `ADDRE017` cell is all `11xx`, the neighbouring cell all low nibbles).
- **OBSERVED (C-6562):** in every discriminating shard the **plain** decode is
  the in-box one (`ADDRE017` 57/57 vs 0/57 stripped; `ADDRE032` 9/9 vs 0/9;
  `ADDRE001` 1,041/1,041 vs 845; `ADDRE014` 3,354/3,354 vs 2,951), so the
  nibble is position in `ADDRE` and `COORD001`, not the POINT marker
  (**C-6563**, narrowing C-2679 to POINT copy-A).
- **OBSERVED (C-6564):** no POINT-style `(0x800|flon, flat)` pair sits at
  `id+6` in any shard; the pair-shaped bytes at `id+8`/`id+12` do not decode
  as a sub-cell of the same coord (EU `ADDRE001`: 2–6 km away), so they are
  token payload. **C-6565:** `ADDRE` carries no fine position; the only
  structured id-adjacent field is the `TT 40 XX YY` point token at `id+6`
  (C-6566), already shown to have no 2D frame (M4.19, C-1964).
- **OBSERVED (C-6567):** `tools/coord.py` now has `packed_units`,
  `decode_point_id`, `encode_point_id` as the single source (tile origin
  52736, bbox origin 52742 kept apart), and `tools/point_census.py`'s
  `fine_position`/`encode_position` delegate to it; both selftests pass and
  agree (C-6568). The census tool is `tools/refer_id_census.py`
  (`--selftest`, 15 checks; C-6569).

## Conclusion

`coord.py` is correct for `COORD001`/`ADDRE` ids with the plain formula and for
POINT copy-A with the point-id formula; the two are separate functions and no
longer share a decode. `docs/format/refer.md` states which files use the
`11xx` nibble and that `ADDRE` has no fine position (`TT 40 XX YY` is the only
candidate field, frame unknown). `COORD001` keeps the plain box origin 52742;
the point-id form keeps the tile origin 52736.
