## Question

With the launcher checks passed (M8.C1c) the applications loop on the
audio-network protocol `CInaiProtocol` ("write failed", C-6886). The
`ze0-0009` DMAC model only stores the registers of the channels the serial
driver uses. Does modelling serial DMA let the protocol proceed?

## Hypothesis

`CInaiProtocol` fails because channel 10 (transmit) never completes.
**Falsifier (from the task):** the protocol still fails with channel 10
completing and its interrupt delivered. **Stop (from the task):** if a
transmit completes only with a reply from the peer, record the bytes and
stop.

## Method

1. Boot `-M ze0,verinf=QSD-302-100,mapdb=16-16,hpbdmac-trace=on` (run T,
   `ze0-0010`, no serial model) and list the register writes of channels
   other than 8/9.
2. Read the interrupt rows of those channels (`ze0_sdhost.py inttable`) and
   disassemble their ISRs.
3. Add `ze0-0011` (below) and boot twice for 330 s host time,
   `-icount shift=0,sleep=off`: run S (`hpbdmac-trace=on`, serial DMA on)
   and control N (`hpbdmac-serial=off`).

```bash
python3 tools/ze0_sdhost.py inttable archive/head256.img | grep -E '0x(b80|bc0|c40|ca0)'
python3 tools/sh4dis.py --image-start 0x3e00200 --va 0x8803e8a8 --count 22 archive/head256.img nk.exe
qemu-system-sh4 -M ze0,dbgmsg=0x880404f4,verinf=QSD-302-100,mapdb=16-16,hpbdmac-trace=on \
  -kernel archive/head256.img -drive if=sd,index=0,file=CARD.qcow2,format=qcow2 \
  -icount shift=0,sleep=off -nographic -serial file:serS.log -monitor none -d unimp -D qS.log
# control N: hpbdmac-serial=off instead of hpbdmac-trace=on
grep -a 'ch10 tx' qS.log | awk '{print $2,$NF}' | sort | uniq -c
grep -a -c 'failed wait response' serS.log
```

## Observations

**O1 — what the serial driver programs (run T).**

| Channel | DSAR | DDAR / DDAR1 | DTCR | DPTR | DCR |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `0xffe41014` (SCIF1 FRDR) | `0xa999a800` / `0xa999a880` | `0x80` | `0x0100` | `0x00052000` |
| 3 | `0xffe43014` (SCIF3 FRDR) | `0xa999c800` / `0xa999c880` | `0x80` | `0x0300` | `0x00052000` |
| 7 | `0xffe47014` (SCIF7 FRDR) | `0xa999e800` / `0xa999e880` | `0x80` | `0x0700` | `0x00052000` |
| 10 | `0xa999a000` (RAM) | `0xffe4100c` (SCIF1 FTDR) | `8` | `0x0001` | `0x00000020` |

Channels 1/3/7 are armed once (`DCMDR = 1`) with both planes; channel 10 is
re-armed for every frame. Channel 12 gets one write (`+0x324 = 1`, its
DPTR). Nothing else.

**O2 — their interrupts.** nk.exe rows `{0xb80, fn 0x8803e654}`,
`{0xbc0, fn 0x8803e6cc}`, `{0xc40, fn 0x8803e7bc}`, `{0xca0, fn 0x8803e8a8}`
(IntrPrio 14, table SYSINTR `0xff`). `fn 0x8803e8a8` writes `0x00080000` to
INT2MSKR `0xff804040` (bit 19), ANDs DINTMR0 `0xffc08814` with `0xfffffbff`
(clears bit 10), writes INT2MSKCR `0xff804044` and returns SYSINTR `0x13`;
`fn 0x8803e654` is the same with bit 1. The SD channels' ISRs return their
own SYSINTR instead (C-6871).

**O3 — the model (`ze0-0011`).** One interrupt output per channel; intc
sources for channels 1, 3, 7, 10 (INTEVTs above, INT2 bit 19, level 14);
INT2 `+0x3c` bit 19 = OR of the six DMAC sources. With `hpbdmac-serial=on`
(default) a channel armed with memory → SCIF FTDR writes its bytes to that
FTDR (the `sh_serial` stub of M8.C10) and completes; a channel armed from a
SCIF FRDR stays armed. `build.sh --check-version 11.1.3`: 15 applied;
`--selftest` 78 passed (one earlier run reported 77/1 and passed when run
again; not reproduced).

**O4 — run S.** 9 675 channel-10 transmits, every one the same 8 bytes
`10 02 02 08 00 10 03 19`. After each, the driver handles the interrupt:
DINTMR0 bit 10 cleared by the ISR (`0x78a` → `0x38a`), DINTSR0 cleared
through DINTCR0 (`wr +0x810 = 0x400`, 2 442 times in the trace), DINTMR0
restored. Channels 1/3/7 log `rx armed … (no peer: stays armed)` once each.
The serial log still has `CInaiProtocol::GetWriteResult! failed wait
response.` (9 674), `WriteData: write failed. Retry...` (4 837).

**O5 — control N** (`hpbdmac-serial=off`): no transmit is made, and the log
has the same lines in the same proportion (9 770 / 4 885 in 330 s). In both
runs `Retry... WriteFile (Write size disagreement. (6:0))` appears twice,
each with `ErrorLogMsg … [ERR_MDL_LCD]`; it is not on the DMA path.

**O6 — the frame.** `10 02` / `02 08 00` / `10 03` / `19`: DLE STX, three
payload bytes, DLE ETX, and a last byte equal to the XOR of payload, `10`
and `03` (`02 ^ 08 ^ 00 ^ 10 ^ 03 = 19`). One transmit per
`GetWriteResult` (9 675 vs 9 674).

## Interpretation

- **FACT (C-6900, C-6901):** the serial driver's DMA set-up and its
  interrupt rows (O1, O2).
- **FACT (C-6902):** with `ze0-0011` channel 10 moves each frame to SCIF1
  and its completion interrupt is taken and acknowledged by the driver (O4).
- **FACT (C-6903):** the protocol does not change: it fails at
  `GetWriteResult … failed wait response` with or without the transmit
  (O4, O5). The falsifier fires for the hypothesis "the protocol fails
  because channel 10 never completes"; the failure is the missing reply.
- **OBSERVED (C-6904):** the frame layout of O6 (DLE STX … DLE ETX + XOR).
  **HYPOTHESIS:** the reply is expected on SCIF1 through receive channel 1,
  which no peer feeds.
- **OBSERVED (C-6905):** `Write size disagreement (6:0)` is the LCD
  module's error, present with and without serial DMA (O5).

## Conclusion

Serial DMA now works in the transmit direction: the audio-network frames
leave through SCIF1 by HPB-DMAC channel 10 and the driver sees each
completion. That does not change what the applications do, because the
protocol waits for an answer from the audio unit at the other end of SCIF1,
and nothing in `-M ze0` plays that unit. This is the task's stop condition
and the edge of F3: getting further needs a peer (the audio unit, the micon)
or at least the receive path, which is the F4 decision (M8.D2).
