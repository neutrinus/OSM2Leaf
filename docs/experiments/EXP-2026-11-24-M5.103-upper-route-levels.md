## Question

Can a from-zero card carry the upper route levels, so that the hierarchical
search (C-10057, C-10265) runs on our data and long routes work?

## Hypothesis

The firmware treats our level-2, -4 and -5 records like the factory ones if
they follow the factory's rules: mesh grouping, node and link flags,
unit 1, and graph shape.

Falsifier: the route search ignores our upper levels. That would show as the
same search stages and the same result with level 2 removed.

## Method

1. **Measure the factory rules.** Read `B48R068R` (level 2) and `LEV3456R`
   (levels 4 and 5) with `tile_relayout`:
   - mesh keys, coordinate scaling and unit-1 selectors per level;
   - which nodes carry `w2 & 0x2000`;
   - which links carry `w1 & 0x2`;
   - nodes on mesh edges;
   - node degrees and parallel links.
2. **Write the levels.** Write levels 2, 4 and 5 from the OSM level-1 graph
   (`sec2_level2`):
   - upper nodes sit at junctions, at mesh edges and where class, type or
     one-way sense change;
   - each upper link is one chain of lower links;
   - unit 1 maps each upper node to its lower node, plus the first lower
     step of every link.
3. **Run in L4.** Use the M5.57 route recipe, zoomed out five times, with a
   map drag per destination, then Map Menu → New Destination. Read the
   `@RPF_srch` serial lines.
4. **Diagnose with gdb.** On a crash or error, set a gdb breakpoint at the
   fault PC or the error print. Dump the registers, the arguments and the
   search lists. The decompile gives the meaning of each field.

## Observations

### Factory rules (static)

- **Mesh grouping (C-10060).**
  - Level 2 groups 4 × 4 level-1 meshes, level 4 groups 16 × 16 level-2
    meshes, and level 5 groups 4 × 4 level-4 meshes.
  - Keys: level 4 `(w0 & 0xff00 | 0x0f, w1 & 0xff00 | 4)`, level 5
    `(w0 & 0xfc00 | 0x0f, w1 & 0xfc00 | 5)`.
  - Node coordinates are `(col * 2048 + x) // S`. This holds for
    8,837 / 8,841 level-4 entries and 14,198 / 14,225 level-5 entries.
- **Upper nodes (C-10061).** A node of level k is a node of level k+1 exactly
  when its `w2` has `0x2000`.
- **Level bit on links (C-10063).** Link `w1 & 0x2` means "this link is also
  on the level above".
  - Factory level 1 sets it on classes 1–7, 10 and 11.
  - Boundary entries never have it.
- **Edge nodes (C-10064).** Only boundary nodes lie on a mesh edge (x or y
  equal to 0 or 0x7ff). This holds for level 1 (688,178 / 688,178) and
  level 2 (54,383 / 54,383).
  - Premap `0x003534d4` flags every node with fewer than 3 links on an edge
    with `0x100000` and treats it as a boundary node.
- **LEV3456R placement (C-10066).**
  - Level 4: slot = `(w0 >> 8) - 0x90`, column = `(w1 >> 8) - 0xc4`.
  - Level 5: shift 10, origins `0x24` / `0x31`.
  - Record `2·col` is unit 0 and `2·col + 1` is unit 1.
- **Graph shape (C-10067).** The London part of the factory level 2
  (34,037 nodes, 85,412 link ends) has no node of degree 1 and no two links
  between the same pair of nodes. Our first level 2 had 2,221 dead ends and
  1,222 parallel pairs.

- **Checker.** `sec2_fields.py --level2` passes the same rules on our tile
  as on the factory tile:
  - one unit-1 entry per level-2 node: 47 / 47 records (factory
    3,214 / 3,214);
  - neighbour count equals the node's link count: 17,194 / 17,194 (factory
    430,438 / 430,438);
  - every link triple names the link target: 48,806 / 48,806 (factory
    1,060,975 / 1,060,975);
  - the level-1 node sits on its level-2 node (¼ scale, ±1): 17,138 / 17,194
    (99.7 %; factory 430,084 / 430,438, 99.9 %). The misses are clamped edge
    nodes.

### L4 results

1. **Crash at `0x3628d2`.** An empty premap entry: a non-boundary level-1 node
   lay on a mesh edge. Fixed by moving such link ends one unit inside
   (1,542 nodes).
2. **Crash at `0x361ee4`.** The find-link at `0x361a08` returned NULL: a
   level-2 node lay on an edge. Fixed by clamping non-boundary upper
   coordinates to 1…0x7fe.
3. **Crash at `0x341df2` (C-10065).** This is the level-2/4 relax at
   `0x36f90c`. The factory `LEV3456R` records point at factory level-2 node
   indices. With our level 2 they must be emptied or replaced.
4. **Level 2 routes (C-10068).** With level 2 from OSM and `LEV3456R`
   levels 4/5 emptied, routes compute with `LEV1OSIDE → LEV2Top →
   LEV1DSIDE`:
   - the first short route;
   - a 12-mile route south-east (destination mesh `a682cdf1`);
   - a route east (`a6a0ce01`);
   - a route further south-east (`a690ce00`).

   Each runs three plural searches and ends with `DISPLAY_ROUTE`. Only the
   level-1 ends of the route are drawn; the level-2 middle is not.
5. **The north-west route fails.** A long route to the north-west ends with
   `MST CAND SELECT ERROR` → `CALC_ERR_OTHER`. The factory tile with the same
   emptied `LEV3456R` routes the same drag, so the fault is in our level 2.
   - gdb at `0x35e98a`: on the factory card the `LEV2Top` path head is a
     virtual goal element (`+4 & 0xf == 2`), which takes the `0x35e688` /
     `0x35e5d0` branch.
   - On ours the head is the origin level-1 element (type 0). The search
     never reached a goal, and `0x35e700` then matches against a list whose
     level-2 fields are `0xffff`.
   - Offline, the destination's level-2 nodes are reachable from the origin
     mesh in our level 2 (523 / 527, directed, within a ±2-mesh window).
   - Not the cause, each tried alone: dead ends, parallel links, one-way
     upper links (all written two-way), more level-2 roads (tertiary, then
     unclassified).
6. **Levels 4/5 from OSM (C-10069).**
   - The card boots with no exception.
   - The boot pre-search (`PRESRCH OSIDE`) runs `LEV1OSIDE → LEV2OSIDE`, then
     `BACK SRCH --> SRCH ERROR`.
   - The third (distance) profile also prints `TOTAL COST OVER FLOW`:
     breakpoint `0x0034246e` in `0x003422ac` fires with the label cost
     `param_1[2] = 0xffffffff`, i.e. a label that was never reached is
     expanded.
   - Long routes do not reach `LEV4Top` yet.

### Pass 2: the north-west route (gdb and RAM dumps)

Card: the pass-1 tile with emptied `LEV3456R` levels 4/5. The north-west drag
is `drag 700 450 100 50` after five zoom-outs.

1. **Memory access.** At a breakpoint gdb reads `E14` for most of Navi.exe's
   heap, for example the search context `0x6236a8` and the mesh grid
   `0x01200700`. QEMU translates through the UTLB only, and WinCE refills it
   in software.
   - Fix: dump RAM (`pmemsave` of `0x08000000` and `0x10000000`, 128 MiB
     each), then translate through the kernel's `SectionTable` with
     `tools/ze0_vmem.py`.
   - Reads match gdb on the pages gdb can read (C-10269).
2. **How `LEV2Top` ends (C-10266).**
   - The search pops its heap until the heap is empty; it does not stop at a
     goal.
   - The destination list at `+0x160` is built afterwards, from the
     destination-side level-1 elements (`0x00369dd0`).
   - Breakpoint `0x00369dd0`, then a RAM dump: on our card every
     destination candidate (mesh cell `0x31`) is reached, with costs about
     `0x48000`. In the whole window, 8,380 of 8,410 nodes are reached.
   - So the level-2 search is not what fails.
3. **Window (C-10268).** For the same drag, our level-2 window is 5 × 4
   meshes and the factory's is 7 × 6. This is not the cause: the route works
   after step 5 with our window.
4. **The cycle (C-10267).**
   - At `MST CAND SELECT` (`0x0035e98a`), the path chain from the head
     element repeats: …343 → 372 … 365 | 364 … 349 | 348 … 343 → 372 ….
   - In the tile, that chain is a one-way pair: links `0xb9` and `0xba` both
     join nodes 0x4f and 0x55 of level-1 mesh `a6bdcdef`. Beside it, links
     `0x78` and `0xdf` both join 0x4f and 0x4b.
   - The path takes `0xb9` in both directions (`0x20b9` and `0xa0b9`).
   - Navi.exe finds links by their far node, so a second link between the
     same two nodes is ambiguous to it.
   - The factory tile has no such pairs: 0 parallel pairs on level 1
     (8,527,448 link ends) and on level 2. Ours had 13,816 on level 1.
5. **Fix and result.**
   - `sec2_writer.split_parallel` puts a node at an interior shape point of
     every later parallel link, or at its midpoint. The second half gets a
     new link id of the same class.
   - The pass runs before and after the edge-node nudge: 6,999 links split,
     0 parallel pairs left on level 1, 6 on level 2.
   - In L4 the north-west route then computes: `LEV1OSIDE → LEV2Top →
     LEV1DSIDE → MST-ROUTE MAKE`, 3/3 plural searches, `DISPLAY_ROUTE`,
     14 km.
   - The south-east control (`drag 300 240 700 240`) still routes: 19 km.
   - As before, only the level-1 ends of the route are drawn.

## Interpretation

The firmware uses our level 2: routes run `LEV2Top` on it, and the falsifier
did not trigger. The static rules of C-10060–C-10067 are needed for Navi.exe
not to crash, and they are enough for short and medium routes.

Pass 1 blamed the `LEV2Top` search for the north-west failure. Pass 2 showed
that the level-2 search reaches every goal. What failed was a cycle in the
level-1 path chain, caused by two links between the same pair of nodes. The
factory never writes such pairs. The writer now splits them (rule C-10267),
and the long route computes.

## Conclusion

Partial. Levels 2, 4 and 5 are written from OSM. Level 2 routes medium and
long London routes in L4 (C-10068, C-10267). Open items:
- drawing the level-2 middle of a route;
- the level-4/5 pre-search error (C-10069).

## Reproduction

```bash
python3 tools/tile_from_zero.py SPLIT --template FACTORY_TILE --cell B48R068R \
  --class-table CLASS.json --country 826 --areas AREAS --urban-fill 0.1 --places \
  --level2 --sec2 --max-plain 80000 --json \
  --lev3456 FACTORY_LEV3456R --lev-out LEV.DAT -o TILE.DAT
python3 tools/sec2_fields.py --level2 TILE.DAT
```

Card: patch `B48R068R.DAT` and `LEV3456R.DAT` in place with
`tools/imgpatch.py --allow-resize` (an emptied `LEV3456R` comes from
`place_lev` with no records). Then run `card_mapcode.py set --from-nk1`.

Route: run `ze0_launch.py --ui-start`, tap the consent screens, then tap ZOOM
OUT (765,200) five times. Drag the map, then tap Map Menu (763,267) and New
Destination (130,377).

The boot pre-search runs before the consent screens, while `ze0_uistart`
holds the gdb port. To break there, one gdb client must do the uistart patch,
clear the key guard and set its own breakpoints.
