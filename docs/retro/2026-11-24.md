## Judge (a): ID collisions are claim-range collisions, not task-ID collisions

The user's observation is right that the board spends time on IDs, but the 24 h
log shows **no duplicate task ID**. The nine repair commits are all
*claim-block* renumberings: M2.27 vs M8.H20a (`9472b7e4`, `5be6cb00`), M2.28 vs
H143 (`66fc4bd0`), M2.28 vs M5.73 (`2805f7a1`), M1.48 (`eedd6797`), M4.28
(`69e55c8e`), M1.53 (`79f8071d`). `tools/board.py next_id()` counts TODO,
history and claims on **`main` only** — its docstring even says "Open PRs can
hold IDs not yet on main: check them too", but the function never does. That is
the root cause: two parallel agents both take the next free block, and the
collision is found at merge time by `claims_check.py`.

Switching to random 6-hex IDs would **not** remove that race (two agents would
still have to consult the same global state, and the check would still miss
open PRs), while discarding the stream/step meaning that `next-id <stream>`,
`Depends`, `Step:` tags and `card_ladder.py steps --check` rely on. The cheaper
fix is to make the allocator see open PRs and to fail at **claim** time, not at
merge time. Prompt change 1 and action 2 below implement that.
