## Judge (a): the sixth cycle-level fix is "no merge without a done-when status"

**The batch worked; the merge step is where the status was lost.** All eight
task PRs are merged or closed, 0 draft PRs, 0 remote `task/*`, CI green on the
tip, and the TL's rule-17 arithmetic (at most one H per three map tasks) held
in both batches — batch 1 was 4 map + 1 H, batch 2 was 3 map + 1 H. The
navigators again paid for themselves (M5.45 alone cost three rounds: an
even-id corpus that did not produce the headline, a false "level-2 splice is
refused" claim, then the C-4463 mechanism itself wrong — physical aliasing at
`5120`, not a shared key).

**But two tasks are `done` in fact and `in progress` on the board.** M5.46
(`14c7498`) and M9.43 (`6375eb1`) both pass their Done-whens on `main`
(re-run this pass: `census: contained=1 intersects=0 outside=62 lattice=63
factory_tiles=45`; `grep -c "python3.13 - <<'PY'"` over EXP-M9.39 `0`;
`selftest --only mutate_refer_poi` OK), both ticked nothing: the M5.46 block
still reads `Status: in progress` and `- Claims: none yet`, M9.43 still
`in progress` with a `Result (driver, awaiting navigator; not merged)` line.
The drivers were told "do not mark done — the TL does", the TL merged, and no
one moved the line. Rule 6 says the **last commit is the work plus `done`**;
the TL prompt says the **subagent** finishes with `Status: done`. The two
rules disagree about who writes the status, and the disagreement is what
created the two ghost lines. The M5.46 driver even left `Claims: none yet`
although its claims are registered (C-4470–C-4479, `claims.md` line 3493), so
a reader of the board cannot find the evidence.

**The claim-block collision recurred, and the numbers now show why.** Seven
drivers took "the next free block" and five landed in C-4420…: M5.43
C-4420-4424, M9.42 C-4430-4439, M3.26 C-4440-4449, M5.44 C-4450-4459, M5.45
C-4460-4469, M5.46 C-4470-4479, M9.43 C-4480-4489. Four renumber rounds (M9.42,
M5.46, M9.43 twice) and one pre-merge reservation (M5.45 moved M5.44's block).
The data rows are unique now (`grep -o '^| C-4[4-5][0-9][0-9]' | sort |
uniq -d` is empty), but the **section headers** are not namespaced: `## M5.45 …
(C-4460–C-4469)` and `## M9.43 … (C-4480–C-4489)` still reserve ranges no
reader can tell apart from PR #229's. Worse for the next cycle: **PR #229

## Judge (c): PR #229's 15-session proposal fits the board, not the evidence

The outside PR (#229, 201 additions, unmerged, claim blocks C-4460–C-4579)
reads the board as "145 open / 66 done / 24 blocked, 63 open blocks ready —
the limit is sessions, not tasks" and splits six serial parents into 12 slices
(M1.34–M1.36, M3.27, M4.42/M4.43, M5.47, M9.46, M4.44/M4.45, M7.6, M9.47),
raising the TL batch ceiling from 10 to **15** with a capacity rule (pairs
count two), a stale-unblock sweep first and a batch ledger.

The evidence supports the direction and not the arithmetic. Direction: the
board does have ready work — the 63-ready figure is its own parse and the
cycle-14 board added 15 tasks (2 archived), 9 of them H (the wrong direction
per rule 17). Arithmetic: this cycle merged **8 tasks in two batches** with 5
concurrent drivers, and the collision tax was five renumber rounds, one H
reservation, and 29 % of drivers hitting C-4420…; the two tasks that were
shipped-but-left-`in progress` were both on `main` and no one noticed, which is
exactly the failure a 15-session batch amplifies. Capacity is not the binding
constraint in the evidence — **claim namespace and merge-status hygiene are**.
The PR's own claim block collides with three merged tasks, which is the
strongest argument against merging it before a reservation rule exists.

What it should change for pairing: the "pairs count two" rule is right and
should be adopted; "at most two tasks per front per batch" is right and
reduces the same-file collisions the TL already avoids by hand; the 15 ceiling
should wait until (i) claim blocks come from a reservation file and (ii) the
merge sets the status. Until then a bigger batch just multiplies renumbers.
Verdict: read as input; do not merge yet; the two capacity rules are worth
porting on their own.
